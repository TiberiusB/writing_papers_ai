# The History of Sensorica: From Seed Value to Open Value Network

Sensorica went online on February 11, 2011, only two years after the launch of the Bitcoin network, as an effort to understand how open networks can engage in material peer production. Sensorica is an OVN, not a traditional organization. Unlike a corporation or a cooperative, an Open Value Network operates through contribution accounting, nondominium property, and legal interfaces that bridge the open community with the market. This piece traces Sensorica's evolution from a Montreal-based experiment to an internationally recognized model: the key milestones, inflection points, and the philosophy behind the structure. For readers interested in alternative economic models, commons-based production, and open innovation, the journey offers both a human story of sacrifice and creativity and a working blueprint worth trusting and adopting.

## Origins: The Seed Value (2011)

The seed value that attracted early collaborators was an optical fiber-based tactile sensor prototype. In a February 2011 post on the Discovery Network blog, Tiberius Brastaviceanu wrote that the seed value establishes a locus of innovation, creation, and production. In Sensorica's particular case, the seed value was a new sensor prototype: a tangible demonstration of capability, know-how, and potential. It drew in makers, engineers, and entrepreneurs who saw in it a shared starting point. From 2011 on, Sensorica joined the participatory and sharing economy movement. Montreal was the established location from the start, as Ettlinger documents in her 2024 case study. The lab would become a physical hub for collaborative work and experimentation.

By April 2011, tensions between open and closed approaches had surfaced. Some members wanted to incorporate and run the venture as a traditional company; others insisted on keeping Sensorica as an open OVN. The compromise was pragmatic and durable: a company was to be created for the Mosquito sensor project, with three members. Hyperion was proposed as a name, but Tactus was chosen instead; the company became Tactus Scientific Inc. Sensorica itself remained an open OVN. Tactus participated as a member with one vote. Brastaviceanu captured the philosophy in a motto: If we can't incite to openness we can't force it. In other words, the new economy comes naturally; we can go with the flow and some will get it later. The motto speaks to the inevitability of increasing transparency and openness in the economy, and a broader migration toward the OVN state. He also wrote that environments like Sensorica are like melting pots where hard shell corporations melt down. Highly collaborative environments, where collaboration is the best strategy, put pressure on closed organizations to open up and become more transparent, because that is how they can do better. It is a matter of adaptation from a competitive to a collaborative environment. A first customer was in sight by April 2011, but the lasting outcome was the consolidation of Sensorica's open identity. The restructuring proved that openness could coexist with commercial activity, as long as the network retained control over its own rules.

## The OVN Model Takes Shape (2012–2014)

By 2012–2014, Sensorica had evolved a three-pillar structure that would become the template for how it described itself to outsiders. Tremblay and Yagoubi, in the first academic case study of Sensorica, describe it as organized around three fundamental pillars: an association, the NPO, for governance; a network of companies for commercialization; and an open, international community for collaborative work and the development of innovation. Sensorica identifies itself as a Fab Lab, a Maker Space, an incubator, and an accelerator that combines elements of all these forms in a new structure called an open value network. That combination allowed it to attract both makers who wanted a space to prototype and entrepreneurs who wanted a path to market, without forcing either group into a traditional firm.

Contribution accounting sits at the heart of the model. Peer production requires recording, evaluating, and redistributing benefits fairly. The Multitude Project blog explains that in order to reward all participants in p2p economic activity, contribution accounting is needed, and that in peer production we see a long-tail distribution of contributions. Contributors are distributed across many roles and levels of involvement; the system must capture that diversity and allocate benefits accordingly. The NRP-CAS (Network Resource Planning–Contribution Accounting System) operationalizes this, providing the infrastructure that makes the OVN legible and governable.

Governance rests on nondominium property, a custodian, and an exchange firm. Nondominium means that assets belong to no single owner but are held in trust for the network. The Canadian Academy for the Knowledge Economy (CAKE) acts as the legal custodian. According to Ettlinger, CAKE pays rent and insurance for Sensorica's material domain, the lab for developing open-source hardware. No one owns Sensorica. The lab is shared. The custodian protects the commons from capture while ensuring that affiliates can access and use the resources according to collectively agreed rules.

An exchange firm is a legal entity that interfaces the OVN with the market: it absorbs liability for products and ensures fair access to the market, preventing any single affiliate from monopolizing commercial relationships. Sensorica first implemented this concept with Tactus, which became Sensorica's exchange firm. Tactus was to interface primarily with funding institutions for research and development, and with the market at a later stage. For a while, Tactus also played the role of Custodian before CAKE took over. Tactus gathered funding for development and eventually for selling devices, including the Mosquito sensor. The Quebec entrepreneurship and Fujitsu prize in 2012, along with the Ingenio Tactus prize and HEC Montréal Concours québécois the same year, were linked to this early commercialization effort. Tactus is no longer in operation; later, BDanConcepts was used for 3D printing services. These awards validated the model at a moment when the exchange firm concept was being tested in practice.

## Institutional Recognition (2015–2017)

June 11, 2015 marked a turning point. Co-founder Tiberius Brastaviceanu was invited to the White House, to the Eisenhower Executive Office, hosted by Jenn Gustetic, Assistant Director for Open Innovation. In a blog post after the visit, Brastaviceanu wrote that Sensorica is a working model in the evolution of open source communities, with infrastructure, methodologies, governance and legal structures adapted to bridge the model to traditional institutions. The presentation contrasted the OVN approach with the XPrize competitive model, arguing that open value networks offered a different path to innovation: one based on contribution and collaboration rather than prizes and competition. The White House event gave Sensorica visibility at a level that few peer production initiatives had achieved.

On June 20, 2016, Jim Anastassiou and Tiberius Brastaviceanu met with the cabinet of Minister Bains on Parliament Hill in Ottawa. The meeting was organized by MP David Lametti, who had followed Sensorica since 2013. The delegation included John Macdonald (Robco), Dhafir Burhan (eVision), and Alexandre Bigot (OuiShare Quebec). Sensorica was seen as a very innovative model for innovation, covering everything from open science and open innovation to peer production. The OVN work fed into ISO/IEC 15944-15, an international standard for open value networks, with Jake Knoppers as Co-Project Editor. Sensorica's practices were thus influencing how organizations worldwide could document and structure value flows. Sensorica's footprint extended to GOSH 2017 in Santiago, Chile, and to the Canadian Science Policy Center and Open Design Hong Kong in 2017, positioning it as a reference in both open science hardware and participatory economy circles.

Academic recognition followed. Tremblay and Yagoubi published the first major case study in 2017, based on five interviews with founders and active members in October and November 2015. They documented the three-pillar structure, the Fab Lab–Maker Space–incubator–accelerator hybrid, and the role of contribution accounting. Freund and Stanko, in 2018, argued that Sensorica has developed one of the most successful operating incentive structures and that its governance is relatively strong, with a clear purpose and matching incentives through open value accounting. These studies gave Sensorica credibility beyond the p2p community, making it a citeable example for researchers and policymakers.

## Scaling and Evolution (2018–2025)

From 2018 onward, Sensorica expanded its footprint. eco2FEST 2018 explored participatory democracy, blockchain governance, housing cooperatives, and urban agriculture, bringing together practitioners from different domains around collaborative solutions. Sensorica participated in the World Forum for a Responsible Economy (WFRE 2017), Movin'On with Michelin (2018), and the Fourth Sector events in 2019 and 2022, which addressed the distinction between public, private, solidarity, and hybrid sectors. The Fourth Sector discussions helped frame Sensorica's place in an ecosystem where traditional boundaries between for-profit and nonprofit are blurred. Current ventures include PEP Master, Decanter Centrifuge, Nondominium, and others. The Montreal lab is administered as a commons: a shared physical space where affiliates contribute and benefit according to the contribution accounting rules. As one Multitude Project post puts it, NRP is for open networks what ERP is for a traditional enterprise.

Challenges arose. Ettlinger notes that the revenue redistributed among the membership is insufficient to support a living wage, and that freeloading—members and non-members using resources without contributing—has been an issue. Sensorica responded by imposing a fee of 5% of revenue from mainstream projects using Sensorica space and resources.

Those observations, however, reflect market-centric metrics. Sensorica's mission is to develop material peer production models that operate in a transnational space, outside the market and traditional finance. The model is designed to transfer some wealth from the market economy into the p2p economy, primarily to fuel the development of the p2p economy, not to maximize revenue within the market. Sensorica builds bridges: legal artifacts and exchange firms that transfer assets and wealth from the market-based economy into the p2p economy. Success cannot be measured in financial revenues alone, which is precisely what the model is designed to work around. The living-wage question, in that light, is a metric mismatch rather than a failure of the experiment.

In recent years, activity has included IASC 2023, REPAIR Art, and the PEP Master Game Play event in 2025. The mission centers on intelligent sensors and sensemaking systems, combining IoT, AI, and p2p approaches. The website claims 80% financial cost savings for entrepreneurs to launch a product on the market and 50% cost reduction for R&D for traditional companies. Ettlinger, in 2024, confirms that Sensorica is still in operation as a CBPP case study. For those who measure impact in patents or revenue, the numbers may look modest. For those who measure it in institutional recognition, academic citation, and sustained operation of a peer production model over fourteen years, Sensorica has delivered a working proof of concept.

## Conclusion

Sensorica has evolved from a 2011 experiment into an enduring Open Value Network with institutional and academic recognition. Key inflection points include the founding, the restructuring that preserved openness, the White House presentation, the Ottawa meeting, the influence on ISO 15944-15, the first academic case studies, and the ongoing scaling of ventures and events. Sensorica remains a stigmergic environment for synergistic open innovation: participants coordinate through signals left in the environment rather than through top-down directives. Roadmap V6 is in development. Partners include hREA, Mikorizal, TaoDAO, OuiShare, and the Multitude Project, extending Sensorica's reach into value-flow protocols, software infrastructure, and p2p philosophy.

Readers interested in learning more, joining, or adopting OVN principles can visit sensorica.co, explore the ventures, and engage with the model. The history of Sensorica is a history of principle, compromise, and persistence: an invitation to trust the approach and consider what open value networks can achieve when openness is defended, contribution is accounted for, and the market is met through legal interfaces rather than enclosure.
